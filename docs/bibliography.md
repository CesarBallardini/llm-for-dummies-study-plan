# Bibliography

Compiled on 2026-10-11 from the study plan. It lists every
**book** and **paper** the plan cites, in the chapters and appendices alike, plus the long-form online articles it uses as reference texts (section 5). Courses,
documentation, tools and datasets are not listed.

## How to read this list

- **Ch.** is the first chapter that cites the work; "A" is Appendix A, the core book shelf.
- **Year** is the year of the edition or version the plan cites. For papers it is the first public release
  (usually arXiv). When the formal venue appeared in a later year, the venue column says so.
- **Publisher / venue** is the publisher for books and the venue for papers. "arXiv" means the work is a
  preprint only.
- **Free online?** has three values:
  - **Free** means the full text is legally and freely available from the official source.
  - **Partial** means part of it is free, such as code, notebooks, chapter summaries or sample chapters.
  - **Paid** means it must be bought or borrowed. **Paid, hard to get** marks a paid book that is also difficult
    to obtain: no open-access edition and no free copy through the usual routes.
- Papers with more than three authors show the first author followed by "et al.".
- Books that exist only as a website, or whose official edition is a website, carry the link to the official site
  in the title. All other works are cited through the official page given in the chapter that uses them.

## 4. Caveats

The plan states the title and the edition for most of these works. It does not always state the year, the
publisher or the authors in full. I filled those gaps from memory and did not check them against the web.
Check these entries before relying on them:

1. **Stewart, Redlin, Watson, *Precalculus* 8th ed.** The year 2024 is copied from the plan. I could not confirm it.
2. **Stitz and Zeager, *Precalculus*.** The plan says 2013 (3rd corrected ed.). A local copy was named 2012.
3. **VanderPlas, *Python Data Science Handbook*.** The plan gives 2022 for the 2nd edition. Please confirm
   that edition exists in print.
4. **Downey, *Think Python* 3rd ed.** I listed O'Reilly as publisher, which is not in the plan.
5. **Burkov, Hefferon, Bekman, Amidi & Amidi.** I listed these as self-published. I did not check that.
6. **Kamath et al., *Large Language Models: A Deep Dive: Bridging Theory and Practice*.** Marked paid and hard to get
   (Uday Kamath, Kevin Keenan, Garrett Somers and Sarah Sorenson; Springer, 2024). Only the Springer preview chapters
   are free, and I found no sign that the full text is open access.
7. **Jurafsky and Martin, *SLP3*.** The plan says "August 2026 release". The year 2026 is the draft date,
   not a print edition.
8. **DeepSeek-R1.** The Nature publication in 2025 is from memory.
9. **Papers with a later formal venue** (RoFormer, Chinchilla, Snell et al., FineWeb2, OLMo 2). I kept the
   arXiv year and flagged the venue only where I was fairly sure.
10. **Garbled or paraphrased titles.** A few titles are the full official titles rather than the shortened
    ones in the plan, for example GSM8K, IFEval, ARC and MMLU. They are worth a spot check.
